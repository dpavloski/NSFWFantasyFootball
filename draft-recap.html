2026 Draft Recap

Grades and full breakdowns from the startup draft, based on where each pick landed relative to 2026 dynasty consensus and how the roster actually holds together.

01
A

Daine Pavloski

profpav

The most complete roster in the league. Gibbs and Omarion Hampton give him two real workhorses, Joe Burrow at 3.2 is excellent QB1 value, and DeVonta Smith, Ladd McConkey, Terry McLaurin, and George Kittle fill out every skill position with no clear weak spot.

Sam Darnold as the backup QB is a legitimate second streamer, not just a name. Nothing about this build needs to break right — it's just deep and correctly prioritized.

07
A-

Ben Kosmalski

kosmalski

Jonathan Taylor next to a fallen Justin Jefferson is the best top-two in the draft. The A.J. Brown (now on New England) plus Drake Maye pairing is a deliberate correlation play on one offense, and T.J. Watt is arguably the single best IDP asset in the league at a spot where only one needs to start.

Docked slightly because the RB room outside Taylor — Bucky Irving, Jaylen Warren, Blake Corum, Woody Marks — is committee depth, not a real RB2.

06
B+

Erin Pavloski

jarvinators

Lamb, Achane, and a fallen Jalen Hurts through three rounds is one of the best stretches of the draft, and Kyren Williams adds a second every-down back. The one real mistake: two defenses drafted (Denver and Detroit), a wasted late pick in a league this deep.

03
B+

Nick Pavloski

mrpav53

Josh Allen locked in early gives him a real, durable edge, and Maxx Crosby plus Kyle Hamilton is the deepest IDP room in the league even though only one starts. The soft spot is running back: outside an aging Derrick Henry, it's Jeremiyah Love (unproven, crowded backfield) and J.K. Dobbins (injury-prone) — no reliable second option.

Since draft day, he's already made a real move: Micah Parsons and Tyler Allgeier came aboard, with Brenton Strange and the Jacksonville defense going out — a notable early add at IDP.

08
B

Mark Pavloski

exsupe

Christian McCaffrey has the highest ceiling of any RB1 in the league. He had a real practice scare in mid-August, but he's since returned to full practice and his head coach has said he'd play if it were a game week — the acute concern has passed, though his injury history is still worth watching.

Kenneth Walker, Nico Collins, Tee Higgins, and Sam LaPorta are a genuinely deep supporting cast, and Bo Nix plus Jordan Love is a solid two-QB answer.

05
B

Keith Day

keith517

Bijan Robinson is the safest pick in the draft, and Lamar Jackson free-falling to 2.10 is the single best value of the entire event. But spending a Round 7 pick on Dak Prescott rather than addressing a receiver room that's genuinely thin — Rashee Rice, an aging Mike Evans, Michael Wilson — reads as a missed opportunity to fix the position that actually needed it.

02
B-

Steve Case

coachcase44

Puka Nacua and Brock Bowers are excellent, and George Pickens gives him a strong WR2. But the quarterback situation is a real weakness: Matthew Stafford is 37 and declining, and Jaxson Dart behind him is unproven — a shakier floor at the position than most of the league solved for.

10
B-

Tony Falbo

tuonni

Chase, McBride, and Nabers in the first three rounds is as good a start as anyone had — McBride at 2.8 is the best pure tight end value in the draft. But the running back room falls off hard after Breece Hall: Javonte Williams, Rhamondre Stevenson, and Isiah Pacheco are all committee pieces, not a real RB2 if Hall misses time.

09
C+

Tyler Olinske

toleet

James Cook and Saquon Barkley to open, plus Patrick Mahomes falling to 11.10, is a genuinely strong skeleton. But he then drafted Alvin Kamara and Travis Etienne — two backs sharing the same New Orleans backfield who'll cannibalize each other's touches — and rostered two defenses on top of that. Good bones, undercut by avoidable roster construction.

04
C+

Robby English

trooptroop

Amon-Ra St. Brown and Ashton Jeanty was one of the best-looking starts in the draft on paper, but Jeanty is currently dealing with an ankle injury and hasn't been cleared — his coach has offered no real timeline, and Week 1 availability is genuinely uncertain.

D'Andre Swift is committee depth behind him, not a real RB2, and two kickers on the roster ties up a bench spot for a position that only ever starts one.
