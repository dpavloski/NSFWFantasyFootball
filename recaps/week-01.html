Opening Weekend Sets the Tone

Ten rosters, five matchups, one family with a lot to talk about at Thanksgiving. Here's everything that happened in Week 1.

The Overview

Tony Falbo's tuonni squad opened the season with the loudest number on the board — 173.06, built on balance rather than one big name, with six different players clearing double digits. It's the kind of week that would normally have The Italian Stallion insufferable in the group chat, except Tony's particular brand of misery only kicks in once the actual Lions disappoint him — so for now, enjoy the rare sight of a happy Tony. It came at the expense of Robby English's Trooptroop, who fell by 35.70, the widest margin of the week. Robby's a state trooper who brings the same energy to fantasy football he reportedly brings to everything else — expect this loss anyway, was probably already circled as inevitable.

On the other end, Mark Pavloski's ExSupe put up the week's low score at 131.34 — losing not to a division-mate on the Packers he definitely didn't over-draft, but to Tyler Olinske's TOleet, who somehow beat the league's biggest Green Bay stakeholder despite living in Green Bay himself. It was Caleb Williams' 41.26 that did the damage, the single best individual performance by anyone this week — no assist needed from Lambeau. Somewhere, Mark is writing this one down on a legal pad next to the couch, right below a note about which bird showed up at the feeder this morning.

The tightest game of the night belonged to Keith Day, who held off Ben Kosmalski by just 21.34 — Michigan pride outlasting Hurrican Gonzalez's chaos in a matchup that saw Bijan Robinson and Justin Jefferson turn in almost identical stat lines on opposite sidelines. And keep an eye on the Pavloski wing of this league: four of the ten rosters share that surname, and Week 1 didn't do much to keep the peace. Nick Pavloski (MrPav53) — the reigning best player in the league and a man who does not need reminding of that fact — beat his own brother, commissioner Daine Pavloski (ProfPav), outright. Erin Pavloski (Jarvinators) picked up a win of her own, while Mark dropped his opener. Prof Pav now has to explain to the family group chat that the one person he most wanted to beat is the one person who beat him.

Nick Reminds Everyone Who Knows Everything

39.66 points from Josh Allen was all the ammunition Nick Pavloski needed to open 1-0 — and to open the "MY NAME'S NICK, I KNOW EVERYTHING" season right on schedule. He beat his own brother and the league's sitting commissioner to do it. Daine got a strong floor game from Jahmyr Gibbs (33.6), but a Pavloski loss to a Pavloski — the exact outcome Prof Pav dreads most, ahead of even a loss to Robby — means a week of second-guessing every roster decision he already made twice.

TOleet 153.96 def. ExSupe 131.34

Green Bay's Own Beats the League's Real Packers Guy

Tyler Olinske — MeatBear himself, an actual Green Bay resident — got the week's best individual score, 41.26 from Caleb Williams, and needed it against Mark Pavloski, the league's true Packers zealot. Mark's Kenneth Walker (34.1) kept things close on the scoreboard, but the week's low team total wasn't enough. Tyler's short fuse presumably stayed in check this time — nobody had to talk him down.

tuonni 173.06 def. Trooptroop 137.36

Two Lions Fans Enter, One Pessimist Leaves Angrier

Tony Falbo's 173.06 was the highest score in the league this week, built on depth rather than one standout — Trey McBride's 24.5 led the way, but six different players cleared double digits. Robby English's 137.36 would have won three of the other four matchups this week; it just ran into a buzzsaw. Both of these guys will tell you they researched every start/sit decision via YouTube breakdown — this week, only one of their homework sessions paid off, and it's the one attached to the guy who's allowed to be happy about it for approximately seven days before the real Lions inevitably ruin his mood again.

Jarvinators 156.62 def. coachcase44 131.90

Erin Stays Positive, Steve Remains a Coach With Players

Christian Watson's 32.7 points powered Erin Pavloski — the league's lone remaining wife, and by unanimous agreement its most pleasant human being — to a Week 1 win. Steve Case got a solid outing from David Montgomery (28.9), which is more than can be said for some of his prior life decisions, but it wasn't enough. He fielded players. They just didn't do enough.

Keith517 162.86 def. Kosmalski 141.52

Michigan Man Survives the Week's Nail-Biter

The closest margin of the week — just 21.34 points — came down to a near carbon-copy showcase from Bijan Robinson (31.3) and Justin Jefferson (31.2) on opposite sidelines. Keith Day's depth around that performance, with four other starters in double digits, was the difference over Ben Kosmalski, a.k.a. Hurrican Gonzalez, whose draft-day kicker habit has become the stuff of league legend even in years he behaves himself. Expect Keith to be insufferable about this in a Michigan-branded way all week.
